## 7. Why Tree Companies Lose Revenue (final)

You're not losing jobs because your crews are slow or your prices are wrong. You're losing them in 8 specific spots between the phone ringing and the check clearing.

### STEP 1 — GENERATE

**Leak #1 — Not enough leads from advertising.**
Most tree guys run Facebook ads themselves, hand it to a nephew, or skip it altogether. One Michigan operator spent $2,500/mo and booked less than 10% — "like throwing money down a hole." (Tree-removal example.)
**Plug:** Meta Ads built only for tree work — removals, trimming, stump grinding, storm calls — aimed at homeowners in your service area.

**Leak #2 — No website conversion, no retargeting.**
A homeowner Googles "tree removal near me," lands on your site, doesn't see a phone number or a real face, and leaves. They never come back. 78% of customers hire the first company that responds. (Removal/trim search-intent example.)
**Plug:** A one-page landing page built for tree work and retargeting that follows them around until they pick up the phone.

### STEP 2 — ANSWER

**Leak #3 — Missed storm calls.**
The phone rings at 11pm with a limb on the roof. You're asleep. It goes to voicemail. 85% of callers who hit voicemail never call back; 67% dial a competitor right then. One emergency removal is worth $1,500–$5,000. (Emergency tree service example.)
**Plug:** AI Receptionist — picks up 24/7, sounds like the office, captures the job before the homeowner hangs up.

**Leak #4 — After-hours calls going to competitors.**
27%–62% of calls to home service businesses go unanswered. At $300/missed call, a 3-truck operation bleeds $10K–$25K/mo. (Trimming/stump grinding calls hitting voicemail during work hours example.)
**Plug:** Missed Call Text Back — homeowner gets a text in <30 seconds asking what they need. They reply. The job stays yours.

### STEP 3 — BOOK

**Leak #5 — Calls answered but never scheduled.**
You pick up, talk for 5 minutes, say "I'll call you back to set something up," and forget. 42% of customer calls die at first inquiry. (Removal estimate request example — caller says "Tuesday works," you forget to put it on the calendar.)
**Plug:** Lead Qualification — job type, address, urgency, scope captured on every call so nothing slips.

**Leak #6 — Slow follow-up.**
Call within 1 minute and you close 7x more often than within 5. Wait an hour, odds drop 400%. While you're up in the bucket, the homeowner is dialing the next number. (Stump-grinding quote example — slow callback loses to the guy who called Tuesday afternoon.)
**Plug:** Appointment Booking — qualified jobs hit your calendar in real time, the homeowner gets a confirmation text.

### STEP 4 — CLOSE

**Leak #7 — Estimates sent but never followed up.**
You drive 30 minutes, walk the property, write the bid, and never hear back. Tree industry avg close rate is 33–50%. Most tree shops do zero follow-up. (Trimming bid sent in May, customer ghosts, books with a competitor in June example.)

### The 2 outputs

### Math (developer-ready)

```javascript
const monthlyAdSpend       = userInput1;
const avgJobTicket         = userInput2;
const callsPerWeek         = userInput3;
const currentCloseRate     = userInput4 / 10;

const CURRENT_MISSED_CALL_RATE       = 0.30;   // Invoca/Aira
const POST_SYSTEM_ANSWER_RATE        = 0.95;   // Kixie
const CLOSE_RATE_LIFT_MULTIPLIER     = 1.25;   // ArboristSite on-the-spot close rates
const ADDITIONAL_CALLS_PER_1K_AD_SPEND = 6;    // Conservative tree-vertical benchmark

const callsPerMonth        = callsPerWeek * 4.33;
const currentBookedJobs    = callsPerMonth * (1 - CURRENT_MISSED_CALL_RATE) * currentCloseRate;
const additionalCalls      = (monthlyAdSpend / 1000) * ADDITIONAL_CALLS_PER_1K_AD_SPEND;
const newTotalCalls        = callsPerMonth + additionalCalls;
const newCallsAnswered     = newTotalCalls * POST_SYSTEM_ANSWER_RATE;
const liftedCloseRate      = Math.min(currentCloseRate * CLOSE_RATE_LIFT_MULTIPLIER, 0.80);
const newBookedJobs        = newCallsAnswered * liftedCloseRate;

const extraJobsPerMonth    = Math.round(newBookedJobs - currentBookedJobs);
const extraRevenuePerYear  = Math.round(extraJobsPerMonth * avgJobTicket * 12);
```
